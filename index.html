What the deadline asks of you

Six yes/no questions, certified by a senior officer

Every three years, a senior officer who can legally bind your organization (your certifier) answers six questions in Ontario’s online portal and certifies that the answers are accurate. A “yes” means you can point to the documents behind it.

The weak spot: proving it

The report is a self-assessment, but your certifier is confirming it’s accurate. The gap we see most is records: new hires get trained, nobody logs it, and three years later no one can show who was trained. Your package includes a training record you keep current, and a walkthrough that names the document behind each answer.

Installed an accessibility widget (accessiBe, UserWay, AudioEye)?

What it does: adds a toolbar visitors can use to change how your site looks. What it doesn’t cover: your policies, AODA training and records, feedback and accessible formats, hiring and accommodation processes, or customer service, which is most of the report. It also doesn’t fix the pages underneath by itself. Keep it if you like it; just don’t count it as your answer.

FAQ

AODA compliance report questions

Ontario publishes free templates. Why pay?

The free templates are good, and we link to them in our guide. If someone on your team has a few days to read the regulation, adapt each template, set up training records and check your website, use them. You pay us to have it done for your organization, finished on a known date, with someone walking your certifier through what each “yes” relies on.

What happens if we don’t file?

Ontario says organizations that don’t file could face enforcement measures, which can include financial penalties. In practice, the ministry works with organizations first: its 2024 annual report lists 1,238 attestation audits (aimed at organizations that missed a deadline, reported non-compliance or never filed), 99% of verification audits resolved as compliant, and 19 Director’s Orders, one with administrative penalties. So the realistic risk is an audit letter and catch-up work, not an automatic fine. It is still a legal obligation, and your certifier signs their name to the answers.

Is this legal advice?

No. We prepare documentation from Ontario’s published requirements and official templates, and explain what they ask for. We are not lawyers. If your situation needs a legal opinion, we’ll say so, and you should speak with an employment lawyer.

Is there still time before December 31?

Yes, if you start by December 5, 2026. We deliver the full package within 10 business days of a completed intake (questionnaire returned and intake call held), with drafts by day 7. Orders after December 5, 2026 are accepted only if we can confirm capacity in writing.

What do you need from us, and how do you handle our information?

A short questionnaire, a 45-minute intake call, and copies of any accessibility documents you already have. We never ask for your portal login, and we don’t need employee health or medical information: we write the processes and blank forms, and your team keeps individual records. We use your information only to deliver your package, keep client records for 7 years for tax purposes, and delete enquiries that don’t become clients within 12 months. Details are in our privacy notice.

We filed in 2023. Do we need to do anything?

Yes. Businesses and non-profits file every three years, so 2026 is a new report. Your documents may also need an update: multi-year plans must be reviewed at least every five years, new staff need training, and many websites have changed since 2023.

Do you file the report for us?

No. Your senior officer creates the portal account, answers the six questions, certifies and submits. We prepare the documents and walk them through each answer first, so they know what supports it. We never log in to your account.

Do you fix our website?

The Full report package includes a check of up to 10 key public pages against WCAG 2.0 Level AA and a fix list ranked by impact. We can quote the fixes separately, or your web provider can do them. For 20–49 employees, the website rules don’t apply, but the same check is available for C$200.

We installed an accessibility widget (accessiBe, UserWay, AudioEye). Are we covered?

Not for the report. A widget adds a toolbar to your site. It doesn’t write your policies, train your staff, keep training records or document your hiring and accommodation processes, which is what most of the six questions ask about. On the website side, a widget doesn’t fix the underlying pages by itself; our website check looks at the pages, not the toolbar. See what a widget does and doesn’t cover.

How do we pay?

Nothing is charged when you start. We confirm fit first, then send an invoice, payable by Interac e-Transfer or bank transfer before work starts. Prices are fixed, in Canadian dollars, plus applicable tax. See the service terms.

We have 200 or more employees. Can you help?

Not with these packages. They are sized for 20–199 employees. Larger organizations have the same report but usually more documents, sites and systems; our free guide and checklist still apply.

Are you connected to the Government of Ontario?

No. We are an independent service. Filing is free, and Ontario’s Accessibility Compliance Reporting Portal is the only place to file.
